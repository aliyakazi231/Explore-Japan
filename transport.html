Transport information

Move through Japan
with confidence.

A plain-language overview of common ways to travel. Fares, route maps, schedules, luggage rules and pass conditions change—always check official operators before travelling.

新幹線

Shinkansen

High-speed rail connects many major cities. Some services use reserved and non-reserved seating. Arrive early and follow the car number on your ticket or reservation.

JR & local rail

Trains

JR and private railways reach cities, suburbs and many regions. Confirm the station name and line: similarly named stations can be far apart.

地下鉄

Subway & metro

Tokyo, Osaka and other large cities rely on fast metro networks. Rush hour can be crowded; let passengers exit first.

IC cards

Suica, PASMO & ICOCA

Stored-value cards often work on local rail, buses and at many shops. Availability, mobile options and acceptance differ by location.

Bus & taxi

Road transport

Buses are vital in Kyoto and rural areas. Taxis are useful for short trips where rail is limited. Check payment methods and late-night availability.

Ferry & flight

Islands & distance

Ferries connect islands and coastal areas; domestic flights help with long distances, especially to Hokkaido and Okinawa.

Local strategy

Choose transport by destination

Tokyo / Osaka

Rail and metro are generally the most practical for central destinations. Use station exits and walking directions carefully.

Kyoto / rural routes

Mix rail with buses, walking and bicycles. Outside larger cities, travel less frequently and plan the return connection.

Official verification: this website intentionally does not display live schedules, fare quotes or rail-pass recommendations. Use the current official rail, transit, airport and ferry operator information for decisions.
